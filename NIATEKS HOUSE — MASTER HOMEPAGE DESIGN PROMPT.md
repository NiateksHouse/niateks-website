# 02 — HOMEPAGE OBJECTIVE

The homepage must answer three questions within the first few seconds:

### 1.

What is NIATEKS HOUSE?

### 2.

What does NIATEKS HOUSE create?

### 3.

Why should a professional brand or buyer work with NIATEKS HOUSE?

The homepage must communicate:

**Design + Textile + Craftsmanship + Manufacturing + International B2B capability**

without becoming corporate or industrial.

---

# 03 — CORE POSITIONING

The homepage should position NIATEKS HOUSE as:

> A design-led textile house combining contemporary design, Turkish craftsmanship and flexible manufacturing for home, hospitality and private-label brands.

Do NOT position the company primarily as:

- A generic textile factory
- A cheap manufacturer
- A commodity fabric supplier
- A mass-market ecommerce store
- A marketplace
- A fashion retailer

---

# 04 — PRIMARY USER

Design the homepage primarily for:

- International buyers
- Home textile brands
- Hospitality companies
- Hotel groups
- Restaurant brands
- Retail brands
- Private-label brands
- Interior designers
- Product developers
- Sourcing professionals

The user may have very little time.

The homepage must communicate credibility quickly.

---

# 05 — SECONDARY USER

The homepage should also work for:

- Designers
- Potential partners
- Distributors
- Trade fair visitors
- General visitors discovering the company

But do not dilute the B2B focus.

---

# 06 — THE EXPERIENCE

The homepage should feel like entering a contemporary European textile design house.

Desired first impression:

> "This company understands design, materials and manufacturing."

The emotional experience should be:

**Natural → Refined → Tactile → Contemporary → Trustworthy → International**

---

# 07 — HOMEPAGE STRUCTURE

Build the homepage in this general narrative order:

```text
1. Navigation

2. Hero

3. Brand / Positioning Introduction

4. Collections

5. Design / Material Story

6. Craftsmanship / Production

7. Private Label / OEM Capability

8. Selected Products or Collection Showcase

9. Brand Statement / Editorial Moment

10. About NIATEKS HOUSE

11. Inquiry CTA

12. Footer
```

You may adjust the order if UX analysis demonstrates a better narrative.

However, do NOT add sections simply to make the page longer.

Every section must have a purpose.

# 09 — HERO

The hero is the most important visual moment.

It must immediately communicate:

**NIATEKS HOUSE = design-led textile house**

The hero should NOT look like:

- A factory
- A stock photo
- A fashion campaign
- A generic ecommerce website
- A technology company
- A hotel website

### Preferred visual direction

Use a powerful editorial textile/environment image.

Possible visual subjects:

- Beautifully designed kitchen textile
- Linen table setting
- Contemporary hospitality environment
- Textile detail in natural light
- Carefully styled home textile composition
- Craftsmanship detail
- Textile product within sophisticated architecture

The image must communicate tactile quality.

---

# 10 — HERO IMAGE RULE

Do not place random text over a busy image.

The composition must contain a clear safe zone for typography.

Use:

- Natural daylight
- Warm neutrals
- Soft shadows
- Tactile materials
- Architectural simplicity
- Editorial photography

The image should look like part of one professional photography campaign.

---

Avoid:

# 33 — WHITESPACE

Whitespace is a primary design element.

---

# 34 — INTERACTION

Interactions should be subtle.

Examples:

- Image scale 1.02–1.04
- Underline animation
- Soft fade
- Small movement
- Smooth navigation transition

Do not add animation merely because it is technically possible.

---

# 35 — MOBILE

Design mobile intentionally.

Do not simply stack desktop sections.

On mobile:

- Typography remains elegant
- Images remain impactful
- Navigation becomes simple
- CTAs remain obvious
- Editorial layouts are recomposed
- Cropping remains intentional
- No horizontal overflow

The mobile homepage must feel designed, not compressed.

---

- Semantic HTML
- Keyboard navigation
- Focus states
- Accessible buttons
- Accessible navigation
- Image alt text
- Sufficient contrast
- Correct heading hierarchy
- Form labels

Do not sacrifice accessibility for visual design.
